## Why your body hates building muscle

Building muscle is hard. Keeping it is expensive (at least, as far as your body's calories and energy are concerned). From an evolutionary perspective, your body doesn't want to carry extra muscle mass for no reason because it costs too many nutrients to maintain.

If you go to the gym and lift the exact same 15 kg dumbbells for 10 reps every single week without pushing harder, your body gets comfortable. It realizes it can handle that stress just fine, so it has zero reason to add new muscle. To actually force your muscles to grow, you have to give them a reason to adapt. You need to present a challenge that exceeds what they're used to.

That challenge is called **progressive overload**.

## What progressive overload actually looks like

A lot of gym-goers think progressive overload means you have to add an extra plate on the barbell every single week. That's a fast track to injury or ego lifting with terrible form.

If you grinded out 4 sets of 5 reps on squats last week, and you load up the exact same weight this week and it's still absolutely challenging, **you are still triggering muscle growth.** As long as the effort is high and you're close to failure, the signal is sent.

However, if that same weight feels like a warm-up set two months from now, you've stopped progressing. That's when you need to turn the dial up.

## 6 ways to progressive overload

Adding more weight on the bar isn't the only way to level up. You've got [options](https://www.reddit.com/r/explainlikeimfive/comments/1vxmxxb/comment/p5q8rp0/):

| Overload method                             | How to do it                                                                             | Best for...                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Add weight                               | Add an extra 1.25-2.5 kg on the bar or grab the next dumbbell up.                        | Strength + hypertrophy                                               |
| 2. Add reps                                 | Keep the weight the same, but turn last week's 8 reps into 10 reps.                      | Hypertrophy + muscular endurance                                     |
| 3. Trim rest time                           | Drop rest times from 3 minutes down to 2 minutes between sets.                           | Cardiovascular + muscular endurance                                  |
| 4. Add sets                                 | Bump your routine from 3 sets to 4 sets for a given exercise.                            | Hypertrophy                                                          |
| 5. Add time under tension                   | Take 3-4 seconds to lower the weight under strict control.                               | Static strength                                                      |
| 6. Level up the variation / range of motion | Switch to a deeper stretch or harder movement (e.g., deficit deadlifts or front squats). | Target strength at difficult joint angles + better muscle activation |

## What exercise science says

1. **Mechanical tension is king:** Lifting heavy-enough stuff [close to failure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12927080/) is what signals your muscles to grow. Old-school myths like "feeling the burn" from lactic acid or getting a huge pump don't actually drive long-term muscle gains on their own.
2. **Volume rules all:** Research consistently shows that total volume (how many hard sets you hit near failure per muscle group per week) is the [#1 variable](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9302196/) tied to muscle growth.
3. **Stop doing 10-second reps:** Controlled reps are great, but intentionally moving at a snail's pace (like 10 seconds per rep) is actually [worse](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25601394/) for building muscle than standard, controlled reps.
4. **Fast reps are fine, just hard to track:** Lifting explosively recruits a lot of muscle fibers, but unless you're an Olympic lifter using specialized velocity-tracking sensors on the bar, it's tough to measure if you're actually getting faster week to week.

## Recommendations based on your goal

Not everyone in the gym is training for the same thing. Here is how you should prioritize progressive overload based on what you want out of your workout:

| Goal                                           | Primary focus                            | Game plan                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Maximize muscle size (hypertrophy)          | Add reps + add sets + add weight         | Pick a solid rep range (like 6-10 or 8-12 reps). Stick with a weight until you can hit the top end of that rep range on all sets with good form. Once you hit that target, increase the weight slightly and repeat the process. |
| 2. Pure strength + heavy lifting               | Add weight                               | Keep reps lower (3-5 reps per set) and rest longer (3-5 minutes). Focus on gradually adding small weight increments to your main compound lifts (squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press) over time.                             |
| 3. Short on time / general fitness + endurance | Trim rest time + add reps                | Keep the weight moderate, keep your rest times capped at 60-90 seconds, and push for extra reps each session. This keeps your heart rate elevated while building muscular endurance.                                            |
| 4. Working around nagging joints / injuries    | Level up the variation / range of motion | Don't chase heavier weights. Instead, use lighter loads with deeper ranges of motion, or add a 3-second lowering phase to make the exercise significantly harder on the muscle without hammering your joints.                   |
